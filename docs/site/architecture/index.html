ETHGlobal Mumbai 2026 · architecture plan v2 · 5 Nov 2026 · comment with 💬

Don't give a robot your wallet.
Give it a budget.

A passkey wallet on Arc that can hand grants to anything: an AI app, a robot, a script. The AI client thinks on 0G, buys from merchants that enforce rules like 18+ with World, and later walks a duck to the till. The wallet never needs to know the grantee is a robot.

1 · WALLET

The foundation

Passkey signup, USDC on Arc, fund / send / receive with no gas shown, grants to any grantee, World verification, an MCP server whose consent screen creates a grant. No Privy-style services.

2 · AI CLIENT

The brain

A 0G-powered agent connected over MCP to the wallet, merchant and robot. It decides what to buy; deterministic wallet code does the paying. Every 0G decision is logged.

3 · MERCHANT

The till

Items, prices and rules (18+, needs approval). Checkout over x402 with our own facilitator on Arc. Later an ESP32 shows the QR and opens a gate.

4 · ROBOT

The body

The duck: its own key on the Radxa, a camera you can watch in the browser, control from the browser or the AI. It pays with a narrower sub-grant.

The one rule: Arc is the authority. Every limit, sub-limit, age rule and human approval is checked on-chain when a payment lands. Our servers coordinate; they can never authorise a payment on their own.

Grants: one mechanism for every grantee

A grant is a signed delegation from the owner's account to an address, with on-chain limits. A grantee can pass a narrower sub-grant on. Each payment is a one-time voucher that our relayer submits, and Arc checks every limit up the chain.

Grant fieldMeaning
DelegateAny address the grantee gives us. The wallet doesn't know whether it's an AI app, a robot or a script
Custodywallet-held: we keep one key per grant, for hosts like claude.ai that can't hold keys · grantee-held: the robot keeps its own key on the Radxa
Limits (caveats)USDC only · native value 0 · per-period cap · expiry · ours: per-payment cap, merchant allowlist, human co-signature above $X
Sub-grantsCan only narrow the parent; revoking a parent kills everything under it

How the robot works

0G never moves the duck. Walking runs on the robot, offline, many times a second. 0G is asked a few times per errand. The robot MCP runs on our Worker, not on the robot.

World, end to end

NeedDesign
18+ ruleVerify once in the wallet → our backend verifies with World → HumanRegistry on Arc. The merchant checks the paying root account. With delegations that's always the owner's account, even when the robot pays. Nothing to forward, no phone per purchase
Age basisNo World proof carries age. "18+" = Orb-verified (World only verifies adults at an Orb): policy, not cryptography. IDKit's 18+ check is a preview needing World's approval
Over-limitAgent Pass device flow → our backend co-signs that one invoice → a caveat on the owner's grant requires it, so it binds sub-grants too
World ID + x402That's AgentKit (with Coinbase, March 2026): unique human, no age, discounts not gates, and its repo was archived on 20 Oct 2026. An optional extra at most

What we know, what we believe, what we don't

Known: we know how to build it

  • Passkey account (HybridDeleGator) and its factory are live on Arc, byte-identical to Base. A passkey-signed grant → AI client → robot → one-time payment chain was simulated against live mainnet state: over-cap payments revert, revoke kills the subtree.
  • Gas without a bundler: our relayer submits everything, ≈ $0.004–0.008 per action.
  • OAuth-consent-as-grant with real MCP hosts (Nod on Stellar, remit on Base); the MCP 2026-07-28 spec and Cloudflare's OAuth provider.
  • One invoice → one payment via IdEnforcer; Arc receipts are final; the ESP32 Ed25519 check needs crypto_ed25519_check.
  • Camera as JPEG over the robot's WebSocket works behind any NAT (Microduck does it); deadman and e-stop patterns.
  • World: no proof carries age; proofs can't be handed to agents; AgentKit is archived.
