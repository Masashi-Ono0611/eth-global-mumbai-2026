ETHGlobal Mumbai 2026 · architecture plan v3.2 · 6 Nov 2026 · comment with 💬 (blocks, diagram nodes, selected text)

Don't give a robot your wallet.
Give it a budget.

A passkey wallet on Arc grants a budget to a robot's chip. One Robot Agent per robot thinks on 0G, pays merchants over x402, and asks a human through World Agent Pass when a purchase is over the limit or restricted. The chip always signs. The wallet never needs to know the grantee is a robot.

1 · WALLET

The foundation

Passkey account on Arc, fund / send / receive with no gas shown, grants to any address, an MCP server whose consent screen creates the grant, Agent Pass approvals.

2 · ROBOT AGENT

The brain

One Durable Object per robot: the AI client. Calls 0G, holds the wallet connection, logs every decision, talks to the chip. Runs with no chip too.

3 · MERCHANT

The till

Items, prices, rules (needs approval, restricted). x402 checkout that accepts both ways to pay. Later an ESP32 shows the QR and opens a gate.

4 · CHIP

The body and the signer

The Radxa on the duck, or a virtual chip in iteration 1. Holds the key the grant is given to and signs every robot payment. Camera and motors on the real one.

The one rule: Arc is the authority. Every limit and every human approval is checked on-chain when a payment lands. Our servers coordinate and relay; they can never authorise, steal or redirect a payment, only delay it.

Grants: one mechanism, the chip is the grantee

A grant is a signed delegation from the owner's account to an address. Here that address is the robot's chip, so the chip signs every payment. The Robot Agent decides; it never holds a spending key.

Two ways to pay

In short: on the grant path the money goes straight from the owner's account to the merchant. For sellers that only accept plain transfers, the chip first pulls the exact amount to itself, then pays. One extra transaction, about a second on Arc, invisible to the user.

PieceHow
FlowWorld IdP's browser flow (not the device flow, which can't carry a nonce): our wallet page asks World to sign in with nonce = the EIP-712 hash of {invoice, amount, payee, expiry} and max_age=0; the owner confirms in World App; World returns an RS256-signed ID token. HumanApprovalEnforcer (built in Arc Studio) RSA-verifies the token with OpenZeppelin RSA.pkcs1Sha256 (modexp, ≈ 150–400k gas, under $0.01) against World's key registered on Arc, and checks iss, aud, exp, sub = owner, nonce = this payment, fresh auth_time, amr includes pop, jti used once. Remaining trust: World's sign-in service and whoever registers its key. Fallback: our backend co-signs
Not usedIDKit 18+ registry (dropped) · AgentKit (archived) · World's wallet as our wallet: "World Money" is a Safe on World Chain only; World's SDK refuses other chains, a Safe can't be a Delegation Framework delegator, and allowances reset after each transaction. At most a World mini app could fund our Arc account

Owner actions and trust

QuestionAnswer
How does the owner send / revoke?The passkey signs a one-time delegation; our relayer (the same code as the facilitator) submits it, ≈ $0.006. This is the pattern MetaMask's own facilitator uses
Break-glassIf the DelegationManager is ever paused: an ERC-4337 user operation via Pimlico's public bundler, gas paid by the account in USDC
Other chains (gas not in USDC)ERC-4337 with a USDC paymaster (as Goblin's wallet did on Base)
Can the relayer steal?No. Signatures fix the exact action and Arc checks every cap; a relayer or bundler can only delay. Hence the fallback
Passkey-native: still a "DeleGator"?Yes. DeleGator is MetaMask's name for their delegation-capable smart accounts. Ours is a HybridDeleGator (own contract, passkey signer); the 7702 fallback points an EOA at EIP7702StatelessDeleGator. Same grants either way

What we know, what we believe, what we don't

Known

  • Passkey account (HybridDeleGator) and factory live on Arc; the whole grant chain simulated against live mainnet state.
  • Circle's Facilitator Service is live on Arc mainnet (x402 v2, EIP-3009, Circle pays gas).
  • No open-source ERC-7710 facilitator ships; we write ~150 lines from PR #1837 + Fireblocks' mechanism.
  • Agent Pass is plain OIDC with no chain involved; deny / expire give no token. Its RS256 token can be verified in an Arc contract (modexp precompile, under $0.01), and the browser flow binds it to one payment via nonce.
  • The chip signs the plan-v3 voucher correctly (bench C1, 20 / 20 checks against MetaMask's kit and live Arc).
  • Goblin's agent stack (Agents SDK, pi-ai, MCP OAuth client, approvals) and its archived wallet (just-in-time top-ups) are proven patterns.
