ETHGlobal Mumbai 2026 · architecture plan v3.4 · Fri 6 Nov 06:30 IST · comment with 💬 (blocks, diagram nodes, selected text)

Don't give a robot your wallet.
Give it a budget.

Vitaquack. A passkey wallet on Arc grants a budget to a robot's chip. One Robot Agent per robot asks 0G what to buy, pays our merchant over x402, and asks a human through World Agent Pass when a purchase is over the limit or restricted. The chip always signs. The wallet never needs to know the grantee is a robot. Build order: docs/spec.md.

1 · WALLET

The foundation

Passkey account on Arc, fund / send / receive with no gas shown, grants to any address, an MCP server whose consent screen creates the grant, Agent Pass approvals.

2 · ROBOT AGENT

The brain

One errand state machine per robot (an Effect service over D1): asks 0G, hands the invoice to the chip over HTTPS long-polling, logs every decision. Runs with no chip too.

3 · MERCHANT

The till

Items, prices, rules (needs approval, restricted). x402 checkout settled by our own facilitator. Later an ESP32 shows the QR and opens a gate.

4 · CHIP

The body and the signer

The Radxa on the duck, or a mock chip in iteration 1. Holds the key the grant is given to and signs every robot payment. Camera and motors on the real one.

The one rule: Arc is the authority. Every limit and every human approval is checked on-chain when a payment lands. Our servers coordinate and relay; they can never authorise, steal or redirect a payment, only delay it.

Grants: one mechanism, the chip is the grantee

A grant is a signed delegation from the owner's account to an address. Here that address is the robot's chip, so the chip signs every payment. The Robot Agent decides; it never holds a spending key.

One way to pay, and why there is no float path

Every robot payment is a chip-signed voucher settled by our facilitator through the DelegationManager, so Arc checks the payee, amount, invoice, caps and revocation at the moment the money moves.

flowchart LR
  a1["Chip signs a voucher<br/>pay merchant · exact amount · invoice"] --> a2["Our facilitator<br/>verify · simulate · redeemDelegations"] --> a3["Arc checks every cap<br/>at payment time"] --> a4["Owner → merchant"]
Grant path (the only path)
Settled byOur facilitator (ported from the S2 spike; no open-source one exists for this scheme)
LimitsDaily cap, per-payment cap, payee allowlist, invoice replay guard, expiry, revocation, approval requirement: all on Arc
Measured cost≈ 505–663k gas ≈ $0.010–0.013 per settle (simulated)
Why no float pathReviewers showed that after a pull to the chip's own address, Arc no longer binds the payee, the invoice or revocation on the second transfer, which breaks the one rule above. Dropped for the event; the later fix is a policy-bound escrow contract, not a chip float

PieceHow
FlowWorld IdP's browser flow (not the device flow, which can't carry a nonce): our wallet page asks World to sign in with nonce = the EIP-712 hash of {invoice, amount, payee, expiry} and max_age=0; the owner confirms in World App; World returns an RS256-signed ID token. Deny, cancel or expiry = no token = no payment
BindingWorld App can't show what is being approved, so our wallet page shows item, amount, payee and invoice, and the approval is single-use for that invoice

On ArcDecided (D15): backend co-signer first. Our backend runs the same checks on World's token (iss, aud, exp, sub = owner, nonce = this payment, fresh auth_time, amr has pop and not mfa, jti once) and co-signs EIP-712; HumanApprovalEnforcer checks the co-signature. At most a World mini app could fund our Arc account

Owner actions and trust

QuestionAnswer
How does the owner send / revoke?The passkey signs a one-time delegation; our relayer (the same code as the facilitator) submits it, ≈ $0.006. This is the pattern MetaMask's own facilitator uses
Break-glassIf the DelegationManager is ever paused: an ERC-4337 user operation via Pimlico's public bundler, gas paid by the account in USDC
Other chains (gas not in USDC)ERC-4337 with a USDC paymaster (as Goblin's wallet did on Base)
Can the relayer steal?No. Signatures fix the exact action and Arc checks every cap; a relayer or bundler can only delay. Hence the fallback
Passkey-native: still a "DeleGator"?Yes. DeleGator is MetaMask's name for their delegation-capable smart accounts. Ours is a HybridDeleGator (own contract, passkey signer); the 7702 fallback points an EOA at EIP7702StatelessDeleGator. Same grants either way
