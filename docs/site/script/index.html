The shape

  • Part 1, the future (screens 1 and 2): everyone gets a robot; it has to pay; the key belongs on the robot.
  • Part 2, what we built (screens 3 to 6): the first version works; a budget; it shops; bigger things ask you.
  • Part 3: the live demo (screen 7), then the close (screen 8).
  • Timing for a 4-minute slot: screens 1 to 6 about 2 minutes, the demo about 1:45, the close 20 seconds.
  • Demo story (limits only, no restricted items): grant 3.00 a day and 1.00 a payment; chai 0.10 pays by itself; a 2.00 thali is over the per-payment cap, so World ID asks you (deny once, approve once); a thali over the 3.00 daily cap makes the robot walk away; revoke, and the next chai is refused by Arc.

PART 1: THE FUTURE

1. Everyone will have a robot

Film: reused. The robot by the front door with a tote bag, the owner tying shoes, morning light.

Soon, everyone will have a robot of their own.

It'll run the errands you don't have time for. And a robot that does things has to pay for them.

You say: "Soon everyone will have a robot of their own. It'll fetch the chai, pick up the parcel, run the errands. And a robot that does things in the real world has to pay in the real world."

2. The problem and the solution, in one

Film: new. In the workshop, a hand offers the robot a bank card. The robot turns its head away, then opens its chest to show its own glowing chip, and the hand withdraws. Plays once and holds on the chip instead of looping.

Not your card. Not a company's wallet. Its own key.

Today a robot can only pay with your card, or from a wallet a company holds for it. Either way, someone else is in control. So the key lives on the robot, your rules live on a public chain, and your money stays in your own wallet.

You say: "Today it has two bad options: your card, which can spend everything you have, or a wallet in somebody's cloud, which can be frozen or shut down. We put the key on the robot itself. Your rules live on a public chain, and your money never leaves your wallet."

PART 2: WHAT WE BUILT

3. What we built this weekend

Film: reused. The robot on the hackathon table at night. Under the title, a strip of real photos from the build: the boards in hand, the wiring, the 3D printer in the car and carried across the hall. If a photo of the printed duck exists by tonight, it replaces the film.

What we built this weekend

The first version works, with real money.

A passkey wallet, a robot with its own key, a budget it can't break, and you in the loop for the big ones. Live on Arc mainnet with real USDC.

You say: "This weekend we built the first version, and it runs on Arc mainnet with real USDC. Here's how it works."

4. You give it a budget

Film: new. At home: you on the sofa with your phone, the robot standing in front of you. Your thumb rests on the phone and a soft light passes to the robot's chest.

You give it a budget, not your wallet.

One tap: three dollars a day, one dollar a go, only at the stall you pick. The money stays in your wallet. The rules sit on Arc, and not even we can bend them.

Enforced on Arc

You say: "You sign in with a passkey, no seed phrase. You add the robot, and you give it a budget with one tap: three dollars a day, one dollar a payment, only at this stall. That budget is a contract on Arc. Our servers can't bend it."

5. It shops for you

Film: reused. The robot at the chai stall; the terminal on the counter glows green.

It shops for you.

Chai at the stall: it decides, its chip signs, Arc settles, the gate opens. Every decision comes with a reason you can read later.

Decided by 0G, every errand kept on 0G Storage

You say: "Chai. The robot's brain on 0G decides to buy, the chip on the robot signs, Arc settles in under a second, and the stall's gate opens. Every decision is logged with its reason, so you can always see why."

6. Bigger things ask you first

Film: new. The robot waits at a counter beside a thali (made from the real thali photo). In the foreground your phone lights up in your hand, thumb hovering.

Bigger things ask you first.

A two-dollar thali is over its limit, so your phone asks. You prove it's really you with World ID and say yes or no. Say no and nothing is paid. Over the day's budget it simply walks away. And one tap switches it off; not even our servers can undo that.

A verified human, with World ID

You say: "A thali is two dollars, over its limit, so it asks. I get a World ID check on my phone, so a real human is behind every big decision. If I say no, nothing is paid. Over the daily budget, it just walks away. And I can revoke the whole thing with one tap; Arc refuses the next payment itself."

PART 3: LIVE, THEN THE CLOSE

7. Now, live

No film. A dark cue card with a button to the wallet. This is where you switch to the app. Can be the last screen instead, if you prefer to end on the demo.

Now, live.

A chai within budget, a thali that asks, and the off switch.

You do: chai (pays by itself, gate opens) → thali 2.00 (World ID asks: deny, then approve) → revoke → chai refused by Arc.

8. Close

Film: reused. Three robots in a Mumbai lane at dusk.

A robot for everyone, with a budget, not your wallet.

  • The robot's agent runs on the robot, not on our servers
  • Several robots on one wallet, each with its own budget
  • Robots paying robots for charging, parcels and rides

You say: "Next: the agent moves onto the robot, several robots on one wallet, and robots paying robots. Vitaquack. Give your robot a budget, not your wallet."

Things we don't say

  • "It can't be blocked." USDC on Arc has an issuer blocklist, and today our servers relay every payment. Nobody can take the money, but if we're down the robot can't pay.
  • "The duck pays by itself." In the demo the chip software runs on a laptop beside the duck; signing on the duck's own board has passed a bench test only. The page says so in its footer.
  • Anything about age. Restricted items are out. If asked: World tells us the verified owner approved this exact payment, just now; it says nothing about age.
